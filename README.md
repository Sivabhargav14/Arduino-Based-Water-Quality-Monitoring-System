## PDCR (pH Detection and Chemical Release System)

PDCR is a water-quality monitoring concept that I developed to explore how technology could be used to monitor polluted water and potentially respond to changing water conditions.

The prototype measures pH and temperature in real time and uses a programmed pH threshold to classify the measured reading of water as acidic, alkaline or neutral.

The larger PDCR concept could be developed further by adding additional water-quality sensors and a controlled treatment mechanism based on the collected data.

## My Experiences 

In Class 10, I started noticing how rivers were becoming increasingly polluted. I also came across news about the Yamuna and the toxic foam appearing on its surface. This made me think about how technology could be used to monitor water quality and respond to pollution.

I came up with the idea of pH Detection and Chemical Release (PDCR), where monitoring units could potentially be deployed near riverbanks, particularly in areas affected by industrial discharge or poor water quality.

The proposed system would collect information such as pH, temperature, and other water-quality parameters. After analysing the data, a future version could use controlled treatment methods to help stabilize water conditions.

In Class 11, I decided to turn the idea into a physical prototype. I independently purchased the components and built the prototype using Arduino, with guidance and advice from my Physics teacher.

## Components Used
Arduino Uno
pH glass rod
DS18B20 temperature sensor
Breadboard
Jumper wires
Laptop with Arduino IDE

## Working
1) The pH glass rod measures the pH of the water sample. 
2) The DS18B20 sensor measures the temperature.
3) The Arduino processes the sensor readings.
4) The readings are displayed on the Serial Monitor in real-time.
5) The measured pH is compared with a programmed threshold (for eg. pH>8 = alkaline).
6) The system displays a classification such as SAFE or UNSAFE based on that threshold.
